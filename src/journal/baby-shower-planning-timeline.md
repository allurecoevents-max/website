> **When to hold it:** many baby showers take place in the last couple of months of pregnancy, when the parent-to-be is still comfortable and travel is easy. Always check what they'd prefer, and be flexible.

## 8 weeks before

- Agree who's hosting and whether it's a surprise.
- Set a budget, and decide who's contributing to it.
- Draft the guest list with the parent-to-be (or their partner, if it's a surprise).
- Choose a date and a rough time: brunch, afternoon tea or an evening gathering.

## 6 weeks before

- Book the venue, or decide to host at home.
- Choose a theme and colour palette. Soft neutrals, florals and pastels are popular, but it should feel like the parent-to-be.
- Book any key suppliers: caterer, cake, florist or balloon stylist.

## 4 weeks before

- Send invitations, with a clear RSVP date.
- Share a gift list or registry link if there is one.
- Plan the food and drink, including non-alcoholic options and any dietary needs.
- Choose two or three activities or games. Fewer is better than too many.

## 2 weeks before

- Chase any missing RSVPs and confirm final numbers with suppliers.
- Order or make decorations, signs, favours and place cards.
- Plan the layout: where the food, gifts, photo spot and seating will go.
- Arrange a photographer, or ask a friend to be in charge of photos.

## The week before

- Write a simple timeline for the day: setup, arrivals, food, activities, gifts, cake.
- Confirm delivery and setup times with every supplier.
- Prepare anything you can in advance: favours, signs, playlists.
- Make a plan for getting the parent-to-be there, especially if it's a surprise.

## On the day

- Start setup early, leaving more time than you think you need.
- Keep a seat comfortable and reserved for the parent-to-be.
- Have someone note who gave which gift, to make thank-you cards easier.
- Leave time for pack-down, so the host isn't cleaning up alone.
