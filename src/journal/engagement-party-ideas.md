## Decide a few things first

- **Who's hosting?** Traditionally a parent or close family member hosts, but many couples now host their own.
- **When?** Most engagement parties happen within a few months of the proposal, while the excitement is fresh.
- **Who's invited?** A simple rule: only invite people you plan to invite to the wedding. It avoids awkward conversations later.
- **Gifts?** Engagement gifts aren't expected. If guests ask, a simple "your company is the gift" on the invitation works well.

## 1. An intimate dinner party

A long, candlelit table for close family and friends, with beautiful florals, place cards and a few speeches between courses. Elegant, relaxed and perfect if your two families are meeting for the first time.

## 2. A garden party or afternoon tea

Light, pretty and easy for all ages. Think floral tablescapes, a dessert table, a signature drink and plenty of space to mingle. Best from late spring to early autumn, with a covered option in case of rain.

## 3. A cocktail evening

A stylish venue, drinks and canapés, a statement backdrop for photos and a good playlist. It suits bigger guest lists, and guests can come and go through the evening.

## 4. A cultural celebration

For many families, the engagement is a major event in its own right, with its own traditions, food, music and dress. Plan it with the same care as the wedding, and make sure both families' customs are woven in.

## 5. A party at home

Your own space, beautifully transformed: balloons, florals, a styled food table and a backdrop for photos. Personal, budget-friendly and completely relaxed.

## Details that make it special

- A **welcome sign** with your names and the date.
- A **photo backdrop** so guests can capture the night.
- A **signature drink** named after the two of you.
- A **memory table** with photos of you both growing up.
- A **guest book** or cards where people leave advice for the marriage.

Whatever you choose, keep it personal. The best engagement parties feel like the two of you.
