## 1. Start with them, not with Pinterest

The best proposals feel like the person being asked. Before you think about flowers or venues, answer a few questions:

- Would they love a big moment, or would they prefer something private?
- Is there a place that already means something to you both?
- Do they want to celebrate with family and friends straight afterwards?
- Will they want photos? (Almost everyone does, even if they say they don't.)

Your answers decide almost everything else. A private person will hate a crowd watching, however beautiful the setup.

## 2. Pick a date with a built-in reason to dress up

The hardest part of a surprise is getting them to the right place, at the right time, looking how they'd like to look in the photos. Anniversaries, birthdays, a "work dinner" or a weekend away all give you a natural cover story.

Popular dates like Valentine's Day, Christmas and New Year's Eve book up early, both for venues and photographers. If you have one of those in mind, start planning several weeks ahead.

## 3. Choose the type of setting

London gives you a lot of choice. The main options are:

- **Private dining room or restaurant:** warm, intimate and easy to explain ("let's go for dinner").
- **Rooftop or terrace:** beautiful views, but you'll need a plan for bad weather.
- **Hotel suite:** completely private, and easy to fill with flowers and candles.
- **Garden or park:** romantic, but public spaces can have rules about setups and photography.
- **At home:** personal and relaxed, and you're not watching the clock.

> **Check permissions early.** Many venues, parks and landmarks have rules about decorations, candles and professional photographers. Always check what's allowed before you fall in love with a spot.

## 4. Always have a plan B

This is London, so plan for rain. For anything outdoors, have a covered or indoor option ready that you can switch to on the day, ideally close by so the cover story still works.

## 5. Decide how it will be captured

A photographer who stays hidden until the moment itself captures the real reaction. That's the photo you'll keep forever. Agree with them in advance where they'll stand, how they'll know when you're about to ask, and how long they'll stay afterwards for portraits.

## 6. Write a simple timeline

Even a small proposal has moving parts. Write down:

1. When the setup starts and finishes.
2. How and when you'll arrive, and who's guiding you there.
3. The signal for the photographer, music or anyone else involved.
4. What happens straight after: champagne, dinner, or friends and family waiting.
5. Who packs everything down, so you can just enjoy the evening.

## 7. Keep the secret

Most surprises are spoiled by small things: a shared calendar, a bank notification, a friend who mentions it. Use a separate email for bookings, keep messages off shared devices, and only tell the people who really need to know.
