## Start with your venue

Your venue already has colours: the walls, the floors, the curtains, the garden. Work with them rather than against them. A grand ballroom with gold details suits rich, warm tones. A glasshouse or garden suits greenery and soft whites. A modern warehouse is a blank canvas for bolder choices.

> **Tip:** take photos of your venue at the time of day you'll marry. The light changes how every colour looks.

## Think about the season

Each season has colours that look natural and are easier to source, especially flowers.

| Season | Palette ideas |
|---|---|
| Spring | Blush, ivory, sage, soft lilac |
| Summer | White, pale peach, dusty blue, fresh green |
| Autumn | Terracotta, burnt orange, deep olive, champagne |
| Winter | Burgundy, emerald, ivory, gold candlelight |

## Choose three to five colours

A simple structure keeps a palette looking calm rather than busy:

1. **A main colour** you'll see most: often a neutral like ivory, cream or white.
2. **One or two supporting colours** for flowers, linens and bridesmaids.
3. **An accent** used sparingly: metallics like gold or brass, or one bold shade.

If you're unsure, start with one colour you love and build gently around it.

## Look for it in real life

Save photos of rooms, outfits, landscapes and flowers that make you feel something, not just other weddings. You'll often find the same few colours keep appearing. That's your palette.

## Test it before you commit

Colours look different on screen. Before you book florists or order invitations:

- Gather fabric swatches, ribbon or paint cards and lay them together.
- Look at them in daylight and in warm evening light.
- Check them next to your outfits, and your skin tones in photos.

## Use it everywhere, lightly

You don't need your colours on everything. They work best repeated in a few places: flowers, table linen, stationery, and perhaps the bridal party. Neutrals do the rest, and keep the day looking elegant rather than themed.
