## Start with three numbers

Before you look at a single venue, agree on:

1. **Your budget**, including how much of it can go on the venue.
2. **Your guest count**, at least roughly. A venue that's too big feels empty; too small feels cramped.
3. **Your date or season.** A Saturday in June is the most competitive; a Friday or a winter date gives you more choice.

These three numbers will rule out most venues straight away, which is exactly what you want.

## Decide what kind of venue feels like you

- **Hotels:** everything in one place, including rooms for guests. Often good value as a package.
- **Historic buildings and private members' clubs:** character and grandeur, usually with strict rules on suppliers and decor.
- **Restaurants:** intimate and food-led, ideal for smaller weddings.
- **Warehouses and blank spaces:** a canvas you style from scratch. Beautiful, but you'll need to bring in more suppliers.
- **Gardens and rooftops:** stunning in summer, but always ask about the wet-weather plan.

## Questions to ask on every viewing

- Is the date available, and can you hold it for a few days while you decide?
- What's included in the price: tables, chairs, linen, staff, cleaning?
- Is there a minimum spend, and what counts towards it?
- Do you have to use their caterer and suppliers, or can you bring your own?
- Can the ceremony and reception both happen here? If not, how far is the move?
- What time do you need to finish, and is there a noise limit?
- Is there step-free access for older or less mobile guests?
- What's the plan if it rains?
- How much is the deposit, and what's the cancellation policy?

> **Read the contract carefully.** Service charges, VAT, corkage, security, extra cleaning and late finishes can add a lot to the headline price. Ask for a full written quote before you commit.

## Think about your guests

A venue can look perfect and still be hard work for guests. Check how easy it is to reach by public transport, whether there's parking or a drop-off point, and where people will stay if they're travelling. For a London wedding, a venue near a station can save your guests a lot of stress.

## Visit at the right time

If you can, view the venue at the same time of day and year as your wedding. Light, noise and temperature all change, and a room that glows on a summer evening can feel very different on a winter afternoon.

## Book the venue first

Once you've found the one, book it. Most other suppliers will ask for your date and venue before they confirm, and the most popular venues can book up well in advance.
