## The short answer

According to [Bridebook](https://bridebook.com/uk/article/how-much-does-a-wedding-planner-cost), the average UK couple spends **£1,543** on a wedding planner, and planners often charge **between 10% and 15%** of the overall wedding budget. Bridebook also notes that a London-based planner will usually cost more than one outside the capital.

That average covers everything from a few hours of on-the-day help to a year of full planning, so the useful question is really: *which kind of help do you need?*

## The three levels of help

| | On-the-day coordination | Partial (guided) planning | Full planning |
|---|---|---|---|
| When it starts | Final weeks | Part-way through | Soon after engagement |
| Who finds venue and suppliers | You | You, with recommendations | Your planner |
| Running the day | Planner | Planner | Planner |
| Relative cost | Lowest | Middle | Highest |

On-the-day coordination is the most affordable because the planner joins near the end. Full planning costs the most because it covers the whole journey: many planners estimate a full wedding takes a few hundred hours to organise.

## How planners charge

- **A fixed fee per package.** You know the price upfront. This is the most common approach for on-the-day and partial planning.
- **A percentage of your budget.** Usual for full planning. The fee grows with the size of the wedding, because a bigger wedding means more suppliers, guests and logistics.
- **A day rate or hourly rate.** Sometimes used for consultations, styling or one-off help.

Whatever the model, ask whether the planner takes commission from suppliers. Some do, and it's fine as long as they're open about it.

## What changes the price

1. **Guest numbers.** More guests means more seating plans, more catering and more coordination.
2. **Location.** London venues and travel take more time to manage. Weddings outside London may add travel costs.
3. **Complexity.** Multi-day, multi-venue and cultural weddings with several ceremonies need more planning time.
4. **How soon the wedding is.** A short timeline means more work in less time.
5. **Design and styling.** If the planner also designs the look and sources decor, that's extra work on top of logistics.
6. **Number of meetings.** Some packages include a set number of meetings; others are unlimited.

## How to compare quotes fairly

Two quotes can look very different simply because they include different things. Before you compare, ask each planner:

- How many hours or meetings are included?
- Are they at the wedding all day, from setup to the end of the night?
- Do they bring an assistant on the day?
- Is supplier sourcing included, or only recommendations?
- Are travel costs extra?
- Do they offer a payment plan?

> **A planner can save you money.** Good planners know fair supplier prices, spot what's missing from a quote, and stop you booking things you don't need. That doesn't make them free, but it often narrows the gap.

## Is it worth it?

If you're both busy, planning from outside London, or simply want to enjoy your engagement, help is usually worth it. If you love planning, on-the-day coordination gives you a calm day without paying for months of support.
