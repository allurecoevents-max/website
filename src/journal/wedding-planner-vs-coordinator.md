## What a full wedding planner does

A full planner is with you from early on, often soon after you get engaged. They help you shape the whole wedding: the style, the budget, the venue, every supplier, and the plan for the day itself. You make the decisions; they do the research, the chasing, the negotiating and the organising.

Full planning suits you if:

- You both work full-time and don't have hours each week to give to planning.
- You don't know where to start with venues and suppliers.
- You're planning something larger or more complex, such as a multi-day or cultural wedding.
- You're planning from abroad or from outside London.

## What an on-the-day coordinator does

An on-the-day coordinator (sometimes called a "day-of coordinator") steps in during the final weeks. You've already booked your venue and suppliers; the coordinator takes your plans, confirms every detail, builds the running order and then runs the day itself, so you, your family and your friends can switch off.

On-the-day coordination suits you if:

- You enjoy planning and have already booked most of your suppliers.
- You don't want a family member "working" at your wedding.
- You want someone to handle timings, setup and any problems on the day.

> **"Day-of" doesn't really mean one day.** A good coordinator starts several weeks before the wedding: reviewing contracts, confirming timings with every supplier and walking through the venue. That preparation is why the day runs smoothly.

## The middle option: guided planning

Many couples want to plan their own wedding but would like an expert on hand to guide them: help with the budget, trusted supplier recommendations, a timeline to keep them on track, and someone to ask "is this normal?" That's guided or partial planning, and it usually includes on-the-day coordination too.

## Three questions to help you decide

1. **How many hours a week can you honestly give to planning?** If the answer is "not many", full planning will save you a lot of stress.
2. **Have you booked your venue yet?** If you have, and most suppliers too, on-the-day coordination may be all you need.
3. **Who will deal with problems on the day?** If the answer is "a friend" or "my mum", think about whether you want them working or celebrating.
